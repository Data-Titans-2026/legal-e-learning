GENERAL LEGAL — Key Concepts
==============================
Small Claims Court
-------------------
For disputes typically under $5,000–$10,000 (varies by state). No attorney required in most states. File in the county where the defendant lives or where the incident occurred. Filing fees are usually $30–$100.

Statute of Limitations
-----------------------
Time limits to file lawsuits vary by claim type and state:
- Personal injury: 2–3 years
- Contract disputes: 3–6 years
- Property damage: 3–5 years
- Medical malpractice: 2–3 years
- Fraud: 3–6 years
Claims filed after the deadline are typically dismissed.

Power of Attorney
-----------------
A legal document authorising someone (the agent) to act on your behalf. Durable POA remains valid if you become incapacitated. Healthcare POA covers medical decisions; Financial POA covers financial/property matters.

Wills & Estates
---------------
Without a will, assets pass by intestate succession laws (typically to spouse, then children, then other relatives). A will must be signed in front of witnesses (usually 2) and may need notarisation. Probate is the court process to validate a will and distribute assets.

Notarisation
------------
A notary public verifies identities and witnesses signatures. Required for deeds, powers of attorney, and many legal documents. Remote online notarisation (RON) is now available in many states.

Mediation vs Litigation
-----------------------
Mediation: voluntary, private, non-binding process where a neutral mediator helps parties reach agreement. Much cheaper and faster than court. Litigation: formal court process with binding judgment. Often takes 1–3+ years.

Finding Legal Aid
-----------------
Low-income individuals may qualify for free legal help from Legal Aid organisations. Bar associations maintain lawyer referral services. Law school clinics offer supervised free or low-cost services.
